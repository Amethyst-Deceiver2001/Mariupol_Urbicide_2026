116 buildings citywide · three independent sources · one recurring pattern
A victims spreadsheet · a Telegram channel · a memorial site — cross-referenced for the first time

The courtyard was a grave. The building was declared “ownerless.”

Three independent sources document siege-era deaths and informal burials in Mariupol: a published named-victims spreadsheet, the full text archive of the Telegram channel mariupolRIP, and over three thousand obituaries from the Ukrainian site memorial.ua. Every record was checked against every seizure event on this project’s property spine — demolition, ownerless-registry inclusion, ownerless designation, reclaim, developer reallocation. The result is 116 buildings where someone was buried in a courtyard or basement, and the building itself later changed hands, one way or another, without the knowledge of the people who lived there. At 71 of the 116, there was no demolition at all — residents simply lost title through a quiet registry entry while a fresh grave still stood in the courtyard.

00 · Method

Three sources never built to agree with each other

Each of the three sources is structured differently and serves a different purpose — none of them was built with the others in mind, which is exactly what makes an address appearing in more than one of them meaningful.

SourceVolumeWhat it yieldedWhat makes it distinct
Mariupol Destruction and Victims Map 4,517 rows 275 confirmed informal burials, 142 distinct addresses a structured spreadsheet with separate fields for place of death and place of burial
Telegram channel mariupolRIP 5,961 messages 284 leads never cited by the spreadsheet above; 72 matched a seized property the very channel the spreadsheet cites — read in full, not through its summary cells
memorial.ua 3,320 obituaries 464 confirmed Mariupol deaths, 7 matched a seized property Ukrainian-language biographical writing — the only source where the address is given the way a family remembers it

Free text — a spreadsheet cell, a chat message, an obituary line — almost never gives the street type: it says “Metallurgov, 47,” not “Metallurgov Avenueпроспект Металлургов, 47.” Rather than guess, the street type was recovered from this project’s own building inventory: if a street-name stem (“Metallurgov,” “Zelinskogo”) matches only one type — street, avenue, boulevard — in the spine, that type is used; if the stem exists under two types at once (Metallurgov itself is one such case — the spine has both a street and an avenue by that name), the row is left unmatched rather than guessed. No false match occurred across the entire run.

memorial.ua is Ukrainian-language, and this project’s address inventory is built from Russian-language occupation-administration documents. Matching the two required a small, hand-verified translation table (Peremohy → Pobedy, Budivelnykiv → Stroiteley, and eight more pairs) — each entry checked individually against the building inventory before it was used.

01 · Zelinskogo Street and Kuprina Street

Two parallel tracks on one street

On Zelinskogo StreetPre-war (UA)вулиця ЗелінськогоOccupation (RU)улица Зелинского, two entirely different seizure mechanisms are running side by side — and both are working through buildings where the dead were buried during the siege.

MechanismBuildingsOutcome
Demolition д.15, 17, 19, 21, 23, 27, 30, 51, 110 DNR “State Defense Committee” Distributive Orders No. 53/56 and municipal administration decrees No. 144/104/149/172 → lot cleared → granted to a developer → new residential complex
Ownerless registry д.1, 3, 13, 19, 30, 33, 87, 94, 96, 98А, 100–108 582 apartments designated “ownerless” flat by flat; the building physically stands, residents stripped of title without demolition

On 15 March 2022, a shell struck directly into the basement of д.98, where residents were sheltering: at least four residents of the building were killed, and a separate victims.memorial record for a Sartana evacuee, Havrylo Barabul, who had taken shelter “in the basement of a high-rise on Zelinskogo Street”«в подвале многоэтажки на улице Зелинского», names the same date and states directly: “at 13:00 on 15 March a tank shell hit the basement, killing 10 people”«15 марта в 13:00 попал танк в подвал, погибло 10 человек» — very likely the same strike, with a higher death toll than the building’s own four-name record. At д.110, an entire family — four members of the Kashiev family — died together in a single apartment (кв.45); three members of the Tolstokorov family remain listed as missing, “bodies not found”«тела не найдены»; a fourth resident, Valentina Pankina, is buried directly in the building’s own courtyard. At д.15, two people died in a fire, one of them — Nina Kotilevskaya — “buried behind the building in the green zone”«похоронена за домом в зелёной зоне».

д.15, 98, and 110 are the only three buildings on the list above absent from the ownerless registry entirely — they were not stripped of title flat by flat, they were demolished outright. Death decided which track a building went down — demolition or the registry — but not whether it went down one at all. The most recent ownerless designations on this same street — 16 apartments at д.108 (December 2025) and 8 apartments at д.13 (February 2026) — were made while a new residential complex was already under construction a couple of hundred metres away.

Kuprina, 9A, by the heat-distribution substation: 13 names, one site

The mariupolRIP Telegram channel separately documents and photographs a mass informal burial site — “by the heat-distribution substation”«у Теплосетей» on Kuprina Street, 9A. Thirteen people are named there: Volodymyr Belenets, Liudmyla Haidai (killed by an airstrike in her own apartment), Valentyna Diakova (Vazianova), Olha Karliani, Mykhailo Kutovyi, Liubov Mykhailova, Fiona Podolian, Alla Soberliak, Oleh Tarasenko, Yevheniia Tereshchenkova, a resident named only as Uiutova, Vladyslav Shumilov, and one further, unnamed record. Several posts separately reference “photo/video of the graves”«фото видео могил» — the site was filmed, not merely described.

At д.19 on the same street, an airstrike killed a father, son and grandmother together — “killed together with his father Sergei Vladimirovich, and grandmother”«погиб вместе с отцом Сергей Владимирович, и бабушкой», all three buried in the courtyard. At д.77 — the exact address where developer “OLIMPSTROY NR” is now building — one person “burned under the rubble of the building”«сгорел под обломками дома».

The 2026-07-12 sweep independently confirmed the already-known burials at д.15 and д.110 and added fifteen new addresses across both streets, none previously in this project’s case-study material:

The new-build at д.276a (“OLIMPSTROY NR”) sits directly between the two heaviest roadside grave clusters, at д.270 and д.307. Of the five burials recorded at д.252 in a single post, four are named — Oleksandr Rozhkov, Mariia Rubina, Klavdiia Lastovinenko, A.A. At Latysheva Street, 35Pre-war (UA)вулиця ЛатишеваOccupation (RU)улица Латышева, at least two deaths are documented specifically from lack of medicine — a category of harm distinct from shelling and airstrikes, directly attributable to the siege blockade: Daria Kvasko “died without insulin”«умерла без инсулина», and Dmytro Oleinyk “died without insulin”«умер без инсулина». The sweep added one further address, д.27, where a two-year memorial post records a death “in his own apartment at Latysheva, 27”«в своей квартире по ул. Латышева, 27».

03 · Eyewitness · the Meduza article

Yaroslav Dema buried his neighbours — five of his addresses are now under construction or in the registry

On 10 June 2022 — before the occupation had even fully consolidated control of the city — Meduza published “Every day you wake up as someone new. Today you’re a gravedigger”«Каждый день просыпаешься и ты кто-то новый. Сегодня ты могильщик»: a feature on Yaroslav Dema, a Mariupol resident who spent the siege burying neighbours and strangers in courtyards and roadside patches across the city. The article is named, addressed, and published independently, before any administrative seizure procedure began — it cannot be read as reverse-engineered to fit a known outcome.

Dema himself lived at Peremohy Avenue, 32/42Pre-war (UA)проспект Перемоги, 32/42Occupation (RU)проспект Победы, 32/42 — the article’s “bank house” — and left from there to bury the dead across the city. One apartment (кв.21) in that building is now in the ownerless registry; the building itself is absent from every demolition list on this avenue, though a dozen neighbouring buildings on the same street were demolished. At Stroiteley Avenue, 160Pre-war (UA)проспект Будівельників, 160Occupation (RU)проспект Строителей, 160, where Dema buried residents in the courtyard and the green zone, 47 apartments are now registered ownerless — again with no demolition; a couple of blocks away, developer “PORFIR” has already commissioned a residential complex, “Rezidentsiya II,” and is building a second. At Solnechnaya Street, 8, an ownerless designation is on record while the two neighbouring buildings, д.1 and д.3, were demolished back in August 2022 under DNR “State Defense Committee” Distributive Order No. 26.

Metallurgov, 96/98: the largest single finding — 121 apartments, and a new complex across the street

The article names the people who died at Metallurgov Avenue, 96/98Pre-war (UA)проспект Металургів, 96/98Occupation (RU)проспект Металлургов, 96/98 — Leonid Soshenko and members of his family, buried by Dema in the building’s own courtyard. That same building now holds this project’s single largest concentration of ownerless-registry apartments at one address — 121 apartments. Neither д.96 nor д.98 appears on any demolition list — the buildings demolished were across the street, on the odd-numbered side (д.79, 81, 85, 87, 89, 91, all under DNR “State Defense Committee” Distributive Order No. 56). It is on the footprint of those six demolished buildings that a new complex, “Leningradsky Kvartal” — 15 apartment blocks, eight already commissioned — is now standing. Geocoding from confirmed anchor points (Russia’s federal cadastral map does not cover annexed territory at all) confirms the new complex sits 50–130 metres from д.96/98, on the cadastral parcel of the demolished buildings, not on the parcel of the building where Soshenko was buried.

The same pattern repeats on Azovstalskaya Street — the single street with the highest ownerless-apartment count in the entire dataset: 537 apartments designated ownerless against fifteen demolished buildings on the same street. This is also where the 2026-07-12 sweep added the most new named evidence — seven addresses: д.7 (Dmytro Karpenko; Viktor Kovalenko), д.9 (Ihor Khrystoforov; Anton Tkachov together with his mother Yevheniia Tkachova), д.19 (Valentyna Kovalenko), д.22 (Anatolii Humeniuk), д.95 (Viktoriia Tonkodubova (Kuvaldina); Vira Tulinova), д.164 (see below), and д.170 (Anatolii Zolotarov).

Precision over drama: д.164 and two different accounts of one name

A mariupolRIP post (b. 24.02.1953, “resident at Azovstalskaya 164, apt. 23”«Проживал Азовстальская 164 кв. 23») gives only the residence of Volodymyr Zviahintsev, killed 22.03.2022 by shelling — “at the Azovstal stadium, on the Left Bank”«На стадионе Азовсталь. На Левом Берегу», in a different part of the city — and says nothing about a burial location, ending instead with an appeal about his missing son. A separate memorial.ua obituary — under the name “Volodymyr Zviahintsev,” same death date, age 69 (consistent with the 24.02.1953 birth date) — tells a very different, harrowing account: five bodies buried together in a shell crater with a hand-written nameplate, and “after Russian forces occupied the city, the bodies disappeared from that site”«після того, як російські військові окупували місто, тіла вбитих звідти зникли» — the family, on returning, could find neither the body nor a new burial site. The matching name, death date, and age make it very likely this is the same person — but that inference is this project’s own, not a confirmed fact, and the memorial.ua account gives no address at all for the crater grave. For that reason it is not pinned to д.164 or to any specific property on the spine. If the two accounts are the same person, this would be the clearest case on file of the occupying authority interfering with an informal grave site itself, not merely with the property around it — but proving that is missing exactly one thing: a named address.

04 · The sweep’s result

116 buildings, most of which were never demolished at all

This project’s earlier grave/new-construction material worked backward from 91 new-build objects to the addresses around them. This sweep inverts the method: starting from every documented informal burial, checked against any seizure event on file, not only demolition. The method change drives the result: demolition — 59 buildings, ownerless- registry inclusion — 54, ownerless designation — 17, reclaim — 11, developer reallocation — 1, expropriation — 1 (the total exceeds 116 because some buildings carry more than one stage). In other words, a full 61% of the buildings in this sweep are ones where looking for a construction site is pointless — the building was simply taken through a registry entry and left standing.

Of the 116, seven streets overlap with the material above (Zelinskogo, Kuprina, Azovstalskaya, Shevchenko, Latysheva), and one address — Metallurgov Avenue, 47Pre-war (UA)проспект Металургів, 47Occupation (RU)проспект Металлургов, 47 — overlaps with the separate “Troianda-M” case study (see section 06 below). The rest is territory this project had not previously documented. Eleven buildings are confirmed by two independent sources at once — a case where the spreadsheet and the channel (or the channel and memorial.ua) name the same address without knowing about each other:

05 · The flagship finding

Lenina Avenue, 127: demolition and developer reallocation, two names never before in this project

The single strongest new lead from the entire sweep comes entirely from memorial.ua, entirely in Ukrainian, and was entirely outside this project’s view until this week. The building at Lenina Avenue, 127Pre-war (UA)проспект Миру, 127Occupation (RU)проспект Ленина, 127 went through exactly the sequence already documented for Nakhimova 82 and the Lenina 104–110 block: demolished, the lot handed to a developer — and two deaths are independently documented at the same address.

This is the same evidentiary shape as two case studies this project has already published: documented deaths at a specific address, independent of the occupation administration, followed by demolition and reallocation of the same lot to a developer, by that same administration. Lenina 127 is a strong standalone candidate for its own case study — what remains is to find the specific land-grant decree, the receiving developer, and, where possible, a third independent source.

06 · Overlap with a published case

Metallurgov, 47: a ninth name

The published case study on Metallurgov Avenue, 47 — where residents organized as HOA “Troianda-M” and lost three times running trying to contest their building’s demolition in occupation courts — already names eight residents killed. Scanning the mariupolRIP channel in full for this sweep surfaces a ninth name never previously in that case: Serhii Kalyna, killed 24.03.2022, named in two separate anniversary tribute posts (2024 and 2025) that state directly he died “at Metallurgov 47”«на Металлургов 47». Neither post gives further detail — no cause, no burial location. The ninth name has not yet been added to the case study or its exhibit: two tribute posts are a substantially thinner source than the eight names already confirmed there, each of which has its own dedicated capture. Treat this as a backport candidate, not a completed fact of the case.

07 · Why this matters

The ownerless registry is part of this picture too, not only demolition

This project’s five previously published grave/new-construction case studies could, by construction, only ever see one scenario: a building was demolished, so check what is being built on its footprint. This sweep deliberately checked for any seizure event, not only demolition — and that is exactly why it found what the earlier method structurally could not: 71 of the 116 buildings here were never demolished at all. People died and were buried in the courtyard of a standing building, and apartments in that same building were later, one at a time, without any demolition, designated ownerless. Across this project’s full dataset, registry inclusion is the single largest seizure stage by far (12,948 events against 580 demolitions) — so the earlier, demolition-anchored method was, by construction, sampling the rarer of the two tracks.

For RD4U and Rome Statute purposes, an informal burial confirms a building was occupied — residents sheltering in basements, dying at their home address — immediately before it was designated “ownerless” or demolished. The multi-fatality shelter events at Zelinskogo 98 and 110, and at Kuprina 9A, directly contradict the official label “ownerless property”: these were occupied, active buildings, not abandoned real estate.
