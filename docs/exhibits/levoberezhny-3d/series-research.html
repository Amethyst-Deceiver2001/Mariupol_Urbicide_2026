Series dossier · Building typology & reconstruction basis

One quarter, seven building types — and the catalogues behind them

The Levoberezhny quarter was not designed building-by-building — it was assembled from the Soviet catalogue of standard-design housing. A citywide building-registry capture has now put real catalogue codes on eleven of its buildings: panel nine-storey slabs of series 1-464Д-83 and 1-439А-41, a brick five-storey of series 1-437, panel five-storeys, point-towers, one fourteen-storey tower, a dormitory, and six civic buildings. The catalogue is what lets a demolished, unsurveyed block be reconstructed at all: where a record fixes a building's floor count and series, the drawing supplies the rest.

The finding

A mixed ensemble, not one catalogue

Seen from the street, the quarter read as uniform — a repeating five-storey grid. The captured record says otherwise: this was a layered ensemble built across two decades, five-storey brick and panel walk-ups fronting the boulevards, nine-storey panel slabs on Ломизова and Комсомольский, ten-storey point-towers on the Азовстальская lot, a fourteen-storey tower at the corner, two kindergartens, a school, a rehabilitation center inside the block.

The confirmed codes divide the housing into families. Series 1-464Д-83 — the Donetsk-regional modification line of the union-wide 1-464 panel programme — accounts for the three nine-storey slabs at Ломизова 9, 11 and 13; three separate registry entries carry the same code, turning a visual "these are identical" call into a documented shared design.15 Series 1-439А-41 covers the single-entrance nine-storey panel slabs at Комсомольский 30 and 36, with 38/2 independently confirmed in the same 1-439А family by its own registry photo caption.15 Series 1-437 — Giprograd's Kyiv brick catalogue, sibling of 1-43810 — is confirmed at 50 лет Октября 20, the pinkish-brick conjoined pair with №22.

For the remaining five-storeys the registry lists a category — «Панельные жилые дома» at 50 лет Октября 4, 6, 8 and Комсомольский 34 — or nothing at all. Where nothing survives, the regional default still applies: the brick five-storey stock of the Ukrainian SSR is overwhelmingly series 1-438 (Giprograd, Kyiv, 1958; «практически не встречается за пределами Украины»)2 or its union-wide sibling 1-447 (Giprogor, 1957).3 That family remains the reconstruction's hypothesis default — applied only to buildings with no confirmed code, and marked as hypothesis wherever it is.

The dataset's own arithmetic still anchors the default: 77 apartments across 4 entrances at 5 floors, four to a landing, at Азовстальская 31 — squarely inside the brick family's documented four-section, 70-to-80-apartment blocks.1

The series is based on a structural scheme of longitudinal load-bearing brick walls with six-metre spans; cross-partitions are set at steps of 2.6 and 3.2 metres. — prawdom.ru, technical description of series 1-4476

The document

What a технический паспорт would have said

No original building passport survives for the demolished blocks. But the series they were built to are documented — down to archival working-drawing albums. The full album of working drawings for one brick-family modification, типовой проект 1-447С-43 (Album I, Part 1, "Drawings"), is preserved and publicly catalogued.14 Combined with the published specifications, it lets us assemble the passport the quarter never kept — every value below carries its source. This sheet documents the brick five-storey family, the reconstruction's hypothesis default; the confirmed panel families follow their own module (panel seam grid at the same 2.8 m storey pitch).

This is a reconstructed passport for the series, not a survey of any individual building. It is the parameter set the 3D reconstruction applies to raise an unresolved footprint into a façade.

Building by building

From footprint to façade

Each footprint (Visicom cadastral polygon) is measured for length, depth and area, then read against its family's bay. Sections follow from length ÷ section spacing; the window grid from the family bay; height from the sourced floor count where it exists, and from the family default where it does not — flagged accordingly.

How the model is built

The reconstruction rules

Window grid. Family window on the family bay; sill 0.9 m; storeys at 2.8 m. Panel families add the seam grid.
Sections. Entrances every ~16 m of length (brick family); single-entrance slabs (1-439А) carry one.
Balconies. Brick family from floor 2; panel 9-storeys from floor 1; dormitory — none.

The honesty rule

Sourced buildings are reconstructed solid, at their recorded height, in their confirmed family. Buildings whose floor count is not yet determined are raised to their family default and rendered as an explicit hypothesis — cooler in tone, capped in amber — never mixed with surveyed fact. Civic buildings are rendered as civic massing, not residential typology. Turn the reconstruction off and everything collapses back to honest placeholder stubs.

Limits

What this cannot tell you

The series fixes typology, not identity. It gives a defensible height, a real window rhythm and a correct storey pitch — but it cannot recover a specific building's exact section mix, its balcony placement, its render or paint, or any later alteration. Two buildings of the same series and length can differ in ways no catalogue records.

Series assignment itself is now confirmed for eleven buildings and photo-grounded for several more; elsewhere it is the strong regional default applied to a footprint that fits the bay. Read the reconstruction as the most disciplined approximation the surviving record allows — and as a frame that tightens, building by building, as registry pages, archival passports and photographs are located.
