56 buildings (48 residential · 8 civic and commercial) · 174 documented dead and missing · one demolition order · zero reconstruction
Levoberezhny district, left bank · Lomizova · 50 let Oktyabrya · Azovstalskaya · Komsomolsky

They demolished the whole quarter. They never built anything back.

A city block on Mariupol’s left bank — bounded by four streets, roughly four hundred metres on a side — carries one of the single densest concentrations of documented civilian deaths and disappearances anywhere on this project’s spine: 174 records across 56 buildings — a full residential quarter of nine-storey panel slabs, five-storey brick khrushchyovkas, a fourteen-storey corner tower, two kindergartens, and a school — cross-referenced against a citywide named-victims dataset and confirmed, address by address, against this project’s own independent reconciliation. A single DNR “State Defense Committee” distributive order cleared the block by the end of 2022. Checked against every new-build record this project tracks, nothing has ever been built here. Not a rumor, not a marketing page, not a rebuilt address. A federal highway-planning decree, approved three years after the demolition, is the closest thing to an explanation on file — and even that explains only why the ground stayed empty, not why the people who lived here were never offered a way back.

Demolition itself followed in stages, precisely dated by residents in their own building chats and by a demolition contractor’s own posted list: the Минстрой ДНР demolition list published 24 October 2022, Azovstalskaya 33 torn down the same day, Azovstalskaya 31 fenced off four days later — “Дом простоял 56 лет”the building had stood 56 years, one resident wrote — Azovstalskaya 7/9/13/15/17 confirmed demolished together by 10 December 2022, and Komsomolsky (Morskoy), 20а fenced off and cleared by 21 December 2022. Clearance proceeded, in another resident’s words, “от угла... в сторону стадиона”corner-first, toward the stadium.

The quarter was identified as a case-study candidate after a satellite-image comparison showed it as one of the very few blocks in the city that is fully razed and still visibly empty — not mid-construction, not fenced for a future project, just gone. Every claim in this exhibit was built to test that first visual impression against the administrative and documentary record, not to confirm it by assumption. A companion block-by-block 3D reconstruction of the quarter, built from the same sourced dataset as this exhibit, lets you walk the block building by building, in a documentary-reconstruction view or a memorial view.

01 · What stood here

A full residential quarter, not a vacant lot with a rumor attached

Four confirmed Soviet standard-design series — independently sourced from domophoto.ru’s building-photography catalogue, cross-checked against resident dashcam footage, a Yandex Street View panorama, and apartment-number testimony — account for the eleven best-documented buildings; the rest of the residential stock is a mix of five-storey brick khrushchyovkas (the areal default for the quarter, evidenced by nearby geo-tagged photographs), point towers, and one dormitory block with no balconies.

Two of the quarter’s residential-looking addresses on the original geographic sweep were not homes at all. Four independent resident video-appeals to Vladimir Putin, recorded 2024–2025 from the quarter’s own foundation pits, converge on the same claim in different words — “48 многоквартирных домов” demolished, plus a small cluster of civic buildings the residents count differently each time (two kindergartens, a school, and a rehabilitation center in one telling; three kindergartens and a school in another). 02 · The casualty tally

One of the most casualty-dense blocks this project has documented

Cross-referencing the Mariupol Destruction and Victims Map’s citywide named-victims spreadsheet against this quarter’s confirmed 56-building footprint (not merely the same street name elsewhere in the city — both Azovstalskaya and Komsomolsky/Morskoy run for kilometres, and house numbers that geocoded outside this specific block were deliberately excluded), extended with resident building-chat testimony naming casualties not yet in the spreadsheet, yields 109 confirmed dead and 65 people listed missing, across 40 of the 56 buildings. 03 · Resident testimony

“Достали 6 тел, трое остались так же возле дома”

Two of this project’s already-mined resident Telegram chats sit directly inside this quarter — including the building chat for Komsomolsky (Morskoy), 20, the single heaviest-casualty address in the tally above. Residents’ own words, in real time, both confirm and extend the spreadsheet-derived tally:

Komsomolsky (Morskoy), 20 · the body count, from a resident who called MChS directly

“Сейчас разговаривала с мчс, сказали достали тела с 28 и 20 дома, в 26 еще не добрались до подвалов”I just spoke with MChS [emergency services] — they said they’ve pulled bodies from buildings 28 and 20, haven’t reached 26’s basements yet. Days later, from the same building: “20 сегодня разобрали, достали 6 тел, трое остались так же возле дома”Building 20 was cleared today, 6 bodies pulled out, three more still lying beside the building.

A follow-up message names four of the dead directly — Oleg (apt. 3), Oksana and Nikolai (apt. 27), and a nine-year-old girl, Anya, described as the daughter of a resident named “Yana” — in reply to a woman searching for her missing mother, Valentina Khilobok (b. 1952, apt. 29), whom the same resident confirms was not among the basement dead. Separately, a woman searching for information about her acquaintance’s mother, who lived at Komsomolsky (Morskoy), 10, was told the body “пролежала 40 суток, после чего завернули в ковер”lay there for forty days, after which it was wrapped in a carpet — with no further information on what happened after that.

04 · The administration’s own words

“Компенсационное жилье строиться не будет”

The strongest evidence that this quarter was left empty is not a satellite photograph — it is the district administration’s own recorded responses to direct questions from residents:

District administration, in response to a resident’s question, 22 June 2023

“Ответ от администрации на вопрос о сроках строительства новых домов на месте снесённого 2го участка. На 2023 год - не предусмотрено”Response from the administration on the timeline for new construction on the site of the demolished “2nd plot.” Not planned for 2023.

Eighteen months later, at a resident meeting captured in a second video-appeal, the head of the Mariupol occupation administration went further than “not planned” — he told residents the compensation-housing track itself was being abandoned in favor of the ownerless- registry pipeline:

Resident video-appeal, January 2025, quoting Oleg Morgun

“На последней встрече с нашим мэром города Олег Валерьевич сообщил, что компенсационное жилье строиться не будет. Нам всем выдадут бесхозные квартиры, но мы не хотим чужое жилье.”At our last meeting with our city mayor, Oleg Valeryevich told us that compensation housing will not be built. We will all be issued ownerless apartments instead, but we do not want someone else’s home.

A separate message, from two months before the June 2023 statement, catches the compensation- housing bureaucracy processing this exact quarter address by address: “мне вчера звонили из администрации по поводу Меотиды 4 (люди уехали до весны), что им готов акт”Administration called yesterday about 50 let Oktyabrya (Meotidy), 4 — people who’d left until spring — saying their demolition certificate was ready. 50 let Oktyabrya, 4 carries two confirmed deaths of its own: Olha Bukhtoiarova (b. 06.08.1939), burned alive in the building’s basement when it caught fire the morning of 20 March 2022, and a neighbour, Halyna Semykina, who died the same day, same entrance, also from smoke.

05 · Compensation, on paper

608 apartments, a resale ban, and a benefit that cancels every other one

Cross-checked against this project’s own record of the occupation administration’s compensation-housing distribution list (built from a separate apartment-level dataset, Постановление «ГКО ДНР» No. 175 / Решение No. 61-1), 46 of this quarter’s 56 buildings are independently listed as the source of one or more “lost” («утраченное») dwellings — 608 apartment-level claims in total, largely separate from the death-and-burial tally above (39 buildings carry both a casualty record and a compensation claim, a genuine but not universal overlap). Heaviest: Lomizova, 15 and Lomizova, 5 (21 apartments each), Komsomolsky (Morskoy), 20А (20 apartments).

The compensation law itself — Закон ДНР № 141-РЗ (18 December 2024), amended by № 161-РЗ (21 February 2025) — makes the terms explicit in its own text, not just in how it is applied: Art. 2 §4 states plainly that receiving this law’s housing benefit forfeits eligibility for any other DNR support measure, and vice versa — the choice is mutually exclusive by law. Art. 2 §3(3) further requires the claimant to sign a formal “обязательство об отчуждении”a binding written commitment to transfer ownership of the destroyed dwelling and its land parcel to the municipality before compensation housing can be issued at all — a precondition, not a formality. Eligible unit size is capped at 33 m² for a single occupant regardless of the lost dwelling’s actual size, and the replacement unit carries a resale ban once title transfers.

A fourth resident appeal, filmed from the quarter’s own foundation pits, ties two of these decree numbers directly to a quantified shortfall: citing Постановление 175 and Решение 61-1, residents state compensation was set at “45 тысяч рублей за квадратный метр”45,000 rubles per square metre against a stated market rate of 120,000 rubles — roughly a 2.7× gap, an independent, first-person figure that corroborates, from the resident side, decree numbers this project had already surfaced from the administration’s own announcement channel.

06 · Renaming as mechanism

The street that carried their address doesn’t exist anymore

Residents were not merely told nothing would be rebuilt — the administrative record of where they used to live was itself altered. One of this quarter’s own boundary streets, Azovstalskaya, was renamed Tulsky AvenueПроспект Тульский — residents in the fourth video-appeal describe the causal link between the two in their own words:

Fourth resident video-appeal, June 2024

“переименовали улицу... оставив нас без прописки, а также открыли себе дорогу под застрой ипотеки на действие наших снесенных домов”they renamed the street... leaving us without registration, and also opened the way for mortgage-financed construction on the site of our demolished homes.

Residents challenged the renaming in occupation courts — and lost, definitively, on 7 August 2025, after fourteen separate hearing dates across three courts (the case bounced back to first instance once for re-hearing). In the plaintiffs’ own account, the Mariupol prosecutor’s office reversed its own position mid-case — having supported the residents’ claim in earlier hearings, it asked the court to reject it in full at the final session. Five months earlier, the administration had already stated plainly why the old name would not return:

Mariupol administration Resolution No. 273, 26 February 2025

“Отмена предыдущего постановления и принятие нового документа связано с адаптацией законодательства ДНР с законодательством РФ”The repeal of the previous resolution and adoption of the new one is due to adapting DNR legislation to RF legislation.

The same resolution keeps “Тульский проспект” permanently, framed as gratitude to “friends from the Tula region” for reconstruction assistance, with a vague promise that a different left-bank street will eventually be named for Azovstal — pending “public hearings” that have not, as of this exhibit, produced a street.

Residents pushed back through official channels too, not just the courts. A message in the Komsomolsky, 20 building chat, September 2023, describes the dispute over mortgage-scheme apartments escalating past the district administration entirely:

Komsomolsky, 20 building chat, 6 September 2023

“Этим всем сейчас занимается прокуратура, так как много жителей хотят жить на своих местах, где было утрачено жилье!”The prosecutor’s office is handling all of this now, because a lot of residents want to live at their own former sites, where their housing was lost!

This is the same evidentiary shape as the residents’ association “Troianda-M,” at Metallurgov Avenue, 47, who lost three times running trying to contest their own building’s demolition in occupation courts. Given the administration’s own June 2023 statement that nothing was planned, the renaming court loss, and the block’s continued emptiness today, neither the prosecutor’s-office complaint nor the renaming challenge appears to have changed the outcome.

07 · Why nothing was built here

A federal highway decree, three years after the fact

The closest thing to an official explanation for this quarter’s continued emptiness surfaced only in 2025, in a planning instrument that has nothing to do with the original demolition order. Приказ № 186-од (Минстрой ДНР, 14 August 2025) approves a new 1.5-kilometre highway overpass and interchange across the former Azovstal port waters and the Kalmius river mouth, connecting to the Р-280 “Novorossiya” federal highway. The decreed route runs directly along this quarter’s own south-east edge — a named 0.74 km segment of Komsomolsky boulevard “от улицы Ломизова до бул. 50 лет Октября” (from Lomizova street to 50 let Oktyabrya boulevard) — the quarter’s own corner-to-corner edge — plus a separate 0.06 km segment of Lomizova street itself.

Approved three years after this quarter’s 2022 demolition, the decree is not the original cause — but it is a documented, dated reason the land was never reallocated to housing in the interim: territory earmarked for a future transport corridor is being held for that purpose, not simply neglected. The planning documents behind the decree were developed by a Russian federal institute, ФАУ «Единый научно-исследовательский и проектный институт пространственного планирования Российской Федерации», commissioned by Russia’s federal Ministry of Construction, not a DNR-local body — the reconstruction planning for this stretch of the former Azovstal territory runs through Moscow. One passage in the decree’s own supporting materials is a striking admission in its own right:

Territory-use documentation, §2.1.3 “Снос объектов капитального строительства”

“В предоставленных данных от Федеральной службы государственной регистрации, кадастра и картографии (Росреестр) отсутствует информация о землепользователях и строениях, расположенных на земельных участках в границах ППТ ЛО”Data supplied by the Federal Service for State Registration, Cadastre and Cartography (Rosreestr) contains no information on landowners or structures within the planning territory’s boundaries.

Russia’s own federal property register, in other words, has no record at all of who owned what on this land — buildings needing demolition were identified from a city-supplied topographic plan and OpenStreetMap instead. Occupation planning bodies are, by their own admission, working from a cadastre that does not know who lived here.

08 · Memorial

What is remembered, and what is not yet identified

On 26 March 2026, the independent exiled Russian outlet ASTRA reported that residents of the “Стадион” district — this quarter’s own colloquial name — held a memorial at a foundation pit for sixteen people who died in a single building entrance during the March 2022 siege. The report’s own stills show an overgrown cleared lot, distant apartment blocks on the skyline, and a small group tying flowers to a burnt tree trunk in the rubble field — no address or building number visible in frame.

Checked against every casualty record already loaded for this quarter, no single building’s count is exactly sixteen. The closest candidate is Komsomolsky (Morskoy), 20 — already the quarter’s heaviest-casualty building, and the only address with an independently documented single-building MChS body-recovery report (§03 above). This project does not track entrance-level (подъезд) granularity, so ASTRA’s sixteen could be a subset of Komsomolsky 20’s recorded dead, or a genuinely different, still-unidentified building. This match is not confirmed — it is recorded here as an open lead, not folded into any tally, pending either a building identification from fuller footage or an independent source naming the address directly.

A different kind of memory survives at the quarter’s north end. Lomizova, 15 — a five-storey khrushchyovka, since demolished — was the rented-apartment filming location for the 1988 Soviet film Little Vera (Malenkaya VeraМаленькая Вера), per a 2012 Ukrainian arts-press profile of the production. The building carries its own casualty record and, separately, twenty-one apartment-level compensation claims — the heaviest compensation load of any address in the quarter. A pre-demolition walkthrough video and a later post-demolition ground-view video of the same block, both independently captured for this project, sit either side of the building’s final months.

09 · What makes this quarter different

Not address-laundering. Not a new complex on someone else’s grave. Nothing at all.

This project has already published two case studies built on the same underlying mechanism seen elsewhere in Mariupol: a building is demolished or its title stripped through the ownerless registry, and a new residential complex rises on or near the same footprint, sold to buyers with no connection to the people who lived there before — Nakhimova, 82 → Chernomorsky, 1B, and the “Rezidentsiya” complex on five courtyard graves at Stroiteley Avenue. This quarter was checked against the same possibility three separate ways — the project’s dedicated new-build-matching corroboration, a raw geographic proximity check against every geocoded newbuild object this project tracks, and the administration’s own June 2023 statement — and all three came back the same way: nothing.

That makes this quarter’s evidentiary shape closer to the citywide death-sites sweep’s finding that most burial-adjacent properties in Mariupol were never demolished at all, just quietly stripped of title while standing — except inverted: here, the demolition happened, on schedule, thoroughly documented, and then simply stopped. No registry sweep of standing apartments, no developer, no reconstruction. A block of the city, cleared of its buildings and most of its residents, held empty for a highway interchange that will not restore a single home.
